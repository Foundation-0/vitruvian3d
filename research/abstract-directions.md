## 1. Reconstructing a shape from its centres

Define T(P), initially for a convex polyhedron containing the origin: partition it into pyramids joining the origin to its faces. take the centre of the largest inscribed ball of each pyramid. take their convex hull. Normalize size when comparing shapes.

General use needs an explicit reference-origin rule and a tie-break if a largest-ball centre is nonunique. A generic pyramid need not admit a ball tangent to every face. The tetrahedra used below do have unique incentres..

For a regular cube, symmetry and the repository's face-pyramid calculation give a regular octahedron. For a regular octahedron, the eight pyramids are congruent tetrahedra whose incentres form a cube. Consequently normalized T has a cube–octahedron two-cycle. This is an elementary consequence of the specified construction, not a novelty assertion.

### Exact anisotropy-erasure deduction

Let P = conv{±a e1, ±b e2, ±c e3}, with a,b,c positive. In its positive-octant tetrahedron, the three coordinate-plane faces force the incenter to be (r,r,r). The remaining face has equation x/a + y/b + z/c = 1. Equating the incenter's distance from that face to r gives

    r = 1 / (1/a + 1/b + 1/c + sqrt(a^-2 + b^-2 + c^-2)).

Reflection gives all eight centres (±r,±r,±r). Their hull is a cube regardless of a:b:c. Thus T discards unequal axis lengths at the level of normalized output shape. The output scale still depends on the lengths. This is not a claim that all input information is lost if scale or the original tetrahedra are retained.

The independent face-area incenter formula is: weight each tetrahedron vertex by the area of its opposite face and divide their weighted sum by total face area. `centre_extraction.py` applies this formula to three axis choices: (1,1,1), (2,1,3), and (0.25,4,1.5). Each matches the closed form to machine precision (reported maximum absolute error zero in this run).

### Angular shear distinguishes the phenomenon

The same script uses generators (2,0,0), (0.4,1,0), and (0,0,3). After centring the eight output points and normalizing their RMS radius, their sorted 28 squared pair distances differ from the cube's spectrum by up to 1.4436580606936333. Therefore this example does not produce a cube. This demonstrates sensitivity to this angular shear.

it does not prove that angles can be uniquely recovered or that every shear survives..

## 2. Can a wave of posture pass through the lattice?

The pinned fold uses row vectors

    L_i(theta_i) = 2 e_i + sqrt(2) cos(theta_i) e_i + sin(theta_i) C_i,
    C_1=(0,-1,-1), C_2=(1,0,-1), C_3=(1,-1,0).

The published fold sets the three angles equal everywhere. Allowing independent cell phases raises a spatial compatibility question beyond collision avoidance.

For cell positions X(n), an assigned edge vector must equal X(n+e_i)-X(n). Hence every square must satisfy the exact necessary condition

    L_i(n) + L_j(n+e_i) - L_i(n+e_j) - L_j(n) = 0.

On a simply connected grid with all loops generated by these squares, closure also permits reconstruction of positions by path integration. Boundaries with periodic identifications need additional global period conditions. This does not by itself ensure sphere non-overlap.

Under a smooth continuum interpolation with fixed global motif orientations, equality of mixed derivatives requires

    L_i'(theta_i) partial_j theta_i = L_j'(theta_j) partial_i theta_j.

Where these derivative vectors are nonparallel, both scalar cross derivatives vanish. Thus this restricted ansatz allows each angle to vary along its own material coordinate, but not arbitrary transverse variation. If all angles equal one common smooth theta(X), the same condition generically forces its gradient to vanish. Exceptional degeneracies must be checked separately.

This is an obstruction for a specific fixed-frame ansatz, not a proof that physical deformation waves are impossible. Spatially varying rotations, elastic deformation, changed connectivity, or defects expand the model and may remove the obstruction. In finite differences, arbitrary local feasibility similarly does not guarantee square-loop closure.

An unsatisfied residual would identify missing degrees of freedom. success would provide a constructive nonuniform configuration. Dynamics and stability remain later questions..
